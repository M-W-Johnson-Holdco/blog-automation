## How Do You Know If Hail Damaged Your Roof If You Can't See It From the Ground?

Most hail damage is invisible from the driveway, which is why a professional roof inspection matters after a storm. Dime-sized hail — the size the NWS forecast this week — rarely punches holes, but it bruises the asphalt mat beneath the granules, fractures the sealant strip, and loosens granules that protect the shingle from UV breakdown (Source: [patch.com](https://patch.com/georgia/atlanta/severe-storms-blast-ga-hail-flash-floods), August 2026). Those bruises trap moisture and accelerate failure over the following months. On the ground, check downspout splash blocks and gutters for washed-out granules, and look at metal surfaces — gutter aprons, vent hoods, and flashing — for fresh dents. If you spot either sign, book an inspection and avoid walking the roof yourself, since foot traffic can create damage an adjuster later attributes to you.

## Does Hail Damage Automatically Qualify for an Insurance Claim in Georgia?

Hail damage may qualify for an insurance claim, but the outcome depends on documentation and the roof's condition before the storm. ## How Do Aging Shingles and Failed Flashing Turn a Hail Storm Into a Denied Claim?

A roof's weakest points — worn shingles, cracked sealant, and failed flashing — are exactly where hail and wind-driven rain cause damage that insurers may attribute to neglect rather than the storm. When granules wear away, the asphalt mat becomes brittle and hail fractures it easily; an adjuster can read that pattern as age. Flashing at chimneys, valleys, and roof-to-wall joints is a common leak entry point, and if it was already loose or improperly installed, water intrusion after a storm often gets classified as a maintenance issue. Poor attic ventilation compounds the problem by baking shingles from below and shortening their life. A professional roof inspection identifies each vulnerability with photo evidence, and when damage is extensive, a full roof replacement resets the clock — installed with GAF or CertainTeed materials backed by manufacturer warranties. ## FAQ

### Should I get my roof inspected if my neighbors had hail but my house looked fine?
Yes — hail falls unevenly, but if your street was in the storm path, your roof likely took impacts even if nothing is visible from the ground. Dime-sized hail bruises shingles without breaking them, and those bruises trap moisture that leads to leaks months later. A professional inspection with photo documentation confirms whether your roof was hit. Skipping it risks missing damage until it becomes a costly interior repair.

### What does dime-sized hail actually do to an asphalt shingle roof?
Dime-sized hail bruises the asphalt mat beneath the surface granules and can crack the sealant strip that bonds shingles together. The NWS forecast this exact hail size for Metro Atlanta this week, and while it rarely punctures shingles, it loosens the granules that shield them from UV damage. Once granules wash away, the shingle degrades faster with every rain. That slow failure is why a post-storm inspection catches problems early.

### How long after a storm do I have to file a hail damage claim in Georgia?
File as soon as damage is documented — most policies expect prompt notice, and delay makes it harder to tie the damage to a specific storm. The NWS warning for your county establishes the date of loss, which supports your timeline. Waiting weeks lets an adjuster argue the damage came from general wear rather than this event. Get a dated inspection report first, then notify your insurer.

### My roof is over 15 years old — will insurance still cover hail damage?
It can, but an older roof invites closer scrutiny of whether damage is storm-related or age-related. Worn shingles fracture more easily under hail, and an adjuster may classify that as pre-existing wear. A documented inspection taken immediately after the storm helps separate fresh hail bruising from long-term deterioration. If your roof is near end-of-life, replacement with impact-resistant materials reduces future claim disputes.

### Why does flashing matter so much in a hail and flash-flooding storm?
Flashing seals the joints where your roof meets walls, chimneys, and valleys — the spots most likely to leak when hail loosens it and heavy rain drives water sideways. The NWS warned of isolated flash flooding alongside this week's hail, which raises the risk of water backing up under compromised flashing. If flashing was already loose or poorly installed, insurers may treat the resulting leak as a maintenance problem. Have an inspector check every flashing joint after a hail event.

### Should I climb up and check my own roof after the storm?
No — walking your own roof risks personal injury and can create damage that an adjuster later blames on you rather than the hail. Instead, check ground-level signs like granules in gutters and dents on downspouts, gutter aprons, and vent hoods. ### Are newer homes in Cherokee County safe from this week's hail?
Newer roofs in Woodstock and the Cherokee County growth corridor generally resist hail better than older housing stock, but they are not immune. Attached gutters, fascia, soffit, and metal vent hoods still dent under repeated dime-sized impacts, and those dents are useful evidence of a hail event. Even a two- or three-year-old roof can lose granules or crack sealant strips. A quick documented inspection confirms whether the newer roof escaped damage or needs attention.

### What's the difference between impact-resistant shingles and standard shingles for Georgia hail?
Impact-resistant shingles are rated to withstand hail strikes that would bruise or crack standard asphalt shingles, making them a strong choice given Georgia's recurring hail threat. They cost more upfront but reduce the chance of storm-related failure and repeat claims. Some insurers offer premium considerations for impact-rated roofs, though terms vary by carrier.
