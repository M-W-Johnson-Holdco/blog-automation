## What do hail and 60 MPH winds actually do to a Metro Atlanta roof?

Hail and 60 mph winds bruise shingles, loosen granules, and lift shingle edges — often invisibly. In Cobb and Gwinnett neighborhoods, wind at that speed exploits any already-loose shingle tab, breaking the seal and letting water track underneath. Hail impacts crush the granule layer that shields asphalt shingles from UV, accelerating aging even when no shingle is missing. Patch reported the storms could bring hail up to a quarter size, frequent lightning, and up to 60 mph winds across the broader Georgia system, with dime-size hail in Monday's metro Atlanta forecast (Source: [patch.com](https://patch.com/georgia/atlanta/sweeping-severe-storms-hammer-ga-hail-60-mph-winds), July 2026). After a storm, check your gutters and downspout splash zones for a buildup of shingle granules — that sediment is one of the clearest early signals of hail impact.

### What are the signs my roof has hail damage after a Georgia storm?

The clearest signs are granules collecting in gutters and downspout splash zones, dented gutter faces and metal flashing, and dark bruise marks or exposed asphalt on shingles. Hail crushes the granule layer that protects asphalt from UV, so granule sediment is often the first clue. You may also find loose shingle pieces on the lawn or dings on soft metal vents. Because bruising is hard to see from the ground, pair your visual check with a professional assessment. Documenting these signs early supports both repair planning and any claim.
