## What Should You Do in the First Hours After a Tree Hits Your Roof?

Move people and pets away from the damaged section first, then treat every nearby wire as live. Georgia Power officials warned during Hurricane Isaias not to touch downed or low-hanging wires — including telephone or TV lines resting on a power line — and to call 911 or Georgia Power if you see a fallen line, since downed wires can hide in debris and wreckage (Source: [AJC](https://www.ajc.com/news/2026/10/hurricane-isaias-affects-georgia/), October 2026). Structural impacts can compromise ceilings and attic framing, so stay out of rooms directly beneath the strike. Once the area is safe, photograph the tree, the roof breach, and any interior water intrusion before anyone moves debris. Those first images become the backbone of your insurance claim.

## When Does a Tree Strike Mean Roof Repair Versus Full Roof Replacement?

A tree strike calls for repair when damage stays localized to shingles and a small section of decking, and full roof replacement when the impact compromises rafters, the deck across multiple planes, or the structural ridge. A single limb puncture over one room often means targeted storm damage roof repair. A full trunk collapse — like the Stone Mountain home crushed during the warning (Source: [FOX 5 Atlanta](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/video/fmc-5i3h0gahgxq8xhud), October 2026) — typically forces replacement because the load fractures framing and flashing beyond spot fixes. Hidden damage matters too: a tree that lands and rolls can tear flashing and loosen fasteners well beyond the visible hole. A documented inspection determines whether your insurer should fund repair or a complete roof system.

## How Does Roof Condition Affect Your Tree-Strike Insurance Claim?

Roof condition directly affects whether an insurer pays your full claim, because adjusters separate storm impact from pre-existing wear. If a tree tears through shingles that were already brittle, or lands on failed flashing, an adjuster may attribute part of the damage to deferred maintenance rather than the storm. Worn flashing at valleys and penetrations is a frequent dispute point — it leaks after impact, but insurers question whether the leak predated the tree. A current, photo-documented roof inspection establishes the roof's condition before the strike and ties the new damage to the dated weather event. ## What Does a Tree-Strike Roof Claim Cost and How Long Does It Take?

A tree-strike roof claim's out-of-pocket cost comes down to your deductible, while the repair timeline depends on structural scope and material lead times. Localized storm damage roof repair moves faster than a full replacement, which requires reframing, decking, flashing, and shingles across the affected planes. Emergency roof repair — a tarp or temporary cover — should happen first to stop water intrusion, followed by the permanent fix once the adjuster signs off. For the gap between your deductible and the project scope, ask your contractor about available financing options so repairs are not delayed by budget timing. Document every expense from the first tarp forward, because reasonable mitigation costs are typically reimbursable under your claim.

## FAQ

### Should I file an insurance claim before or after I get my roof inspected?
Get your roof inspected first, then file — a documented inspection gives you facts instead of guesses. An inspection report with dated photos lets you describe the damage accurately and attach evidence from the start. Filing blind risks understating the scope, which can shortchange your payout. For a tree strike tied to a named event like the October 2026 wind warning across Cobb and DeKalb, pairing the inspection with the storm date strengthens the file. A professional roof inspection also catches hidden flashing and decking damage an untrained eye misses.

### What should I photograph after a tree falls on my roof in Metro Atlanta?
Photograph the fallen tree, the roof breach, torn flashing, and any interior water stains before anyone touches the debris. Wide shots establish the whole scene; close-ups show punctures, cracked decking, and lifted shingles. Include the tree's base if wind or saturated ground caused the fall, since that supports a storm attribution. Capture ceilings, attic framing, and wet insulation inside the home. Time-stamped images taken close to the event date — like the Buckhead tree that fell on North Fulton Drive during Isaias — carry the most weight with adjusters.

### Does homeowners insurance in Georgia cover a tree hitting my roof?
Most Georgia homeowners policies cover roof damage from a tree falling due to wind or storm, though coverage specifics vary by policy. The key is tying the damage to a sudden, dated event rather than gradual wear. An emergency declaration was approved for Georgia during Isaias, underscoring the recognized storm impact across the state. Your deductible applies, and the insurer may scrutinize the roof's pre-strike condition. A documented inspection helps separate storm damage from any pre-existing issues.

### How fast should I cover a hole in my roof after a tree strike?
Cover an exposed hole as soon as it is safe to do so from the ground — water intrusion worsens damage every hour it stays open. Temporary tarping is mitigation your policy generally expects, and failing to act can reduce your payout if interior damage spreads. Never climb onto a compromised roof, especially with downed wires nearby; Georgia Power warned that fallen lines can hide in storm debris. Hire emergency roof repair if the breach is large or structural. Keep receipts for every mitigation step.

### Who removes the tree versus who fixes the roof after a collapse?
Tree removal and roof repair are usually two separate services, and your claim can cover both when a storm causes the damage. A tree service or crane crew extracts the trunk safely, particularly after a full collapse like the Stone Mountain home crushed during the wind warning. A licensed roofing contractor then assesses structural and decking damage and completes the permanent repair or roof replacement. Coordinating the two matters so the roofer can inspect the deck immediately after the tree is clear. Document the roof the moment the tree is removed, before any temporary cover goes on.

### Will an old roof get my tree-damage claim denied in Atlanta?
An old roof alone rarely triggers an outright denial, but worn condition can lead an adjuster to attribute part of the damage to wear rather than the tree. This is common with aged flashing and brittle shingles that leak after impact. A pre-loss or post-event inspection report helps prove the tree — not age — caused the breach. If your roof was near the end of its service life, the insurer may apply depreciation to that portion. Replacing a failing roof after a covered strike resets that exposure for future storms.

### What flashing and ventilation damage should I check after a tree impact?
Check valley flashing, chimney and vent penetrations, and ridge vents — these are where tree impacts commonly tear the waterproof layer. Damaged flashing leaks even when shingles look intact, and insurers often dispute whether that leak is storm-related or old. Crushed ridge vents compromise attic airflow, which affects moisture and shingle life. A limb that lands and slides can bend flashing at the valleys well away from the visible breach. Flag any bent, lifted, or missing metal in your inspection so it is included in the claim scope.

Selecting a licensed and insured roofing contractor with manufacturer-backed workmanship protects your warranty. Verify the contractor documents damage thoroughly and works directly with your adjuster. Ask how they handle the claim timeline and material lead times before signing.
