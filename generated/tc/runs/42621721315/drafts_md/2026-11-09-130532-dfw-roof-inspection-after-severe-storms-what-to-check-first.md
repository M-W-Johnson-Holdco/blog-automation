## What Should DFW Homeowners Inspect on Their Roof After the Storm?

Start with a safe, ground-level walk-around looking for missing or curled shingles, dented metal flashing, bent gutters, and shingle granules collecting at downspouts. The recent North Texas front brought damaging wind gusts up to 60 mph plus hail up to the size of quarters, both of which strip protective granules and loosen fasteners (Source: [Fox 4](https://www.fox4news.com/weather/dallas-weather-severe-storms-target-dfw-overnight-ahead-election-day), November 2026). In older neighborhoods like Richardson and Garland, where many ranch-style homes still carry original or single-layer asphalt roofs, quarter-size hail commonly fractures aging shingles that already lost flexibility to Texas heat. Check interior ceilings and attic decking for fresh water stains too, since flash flooding and wind-driven rain exploit even small openings. Document everything with timestamped photos — stay off the roof itself and leave the elevated inspection to a professional.

## How Do Aging Shingles and Failed Flashing Turn Into Denied Claims?

Insurers often deny storm claims when they attribute damage to wear, poor maintenance, or installation defects rather than the storm itself — and aging roof components make that argument easy for an adjuster. Cracked, brittle shingles that lost granules to years of Texas sun can look like age-related deterioration even when hail caused the fresh fractures. Failed flashing at chimneys, skylights, and roof-to-wall joints — common on 1990s-era homes in Bedford and Carrollton — lets water in and gets labeled a maintenance issue instead of wind damage. Deferred repairs on a roof past 15 years give insurers room to reduce or reject a payout. A documented professional roof inspection draws the line between pre-existing wear and new storm damage, and timely roof repair or roof replacement removes the vulnerabilities that trigger denials. Act in a clear sequence: ensure safety first, document second, then call a licensed contractor before your insurer. ## FAQ

### How soon after a DFW storm should I get my roof inspected?
Schedule a professional roof inspection within one to two weeks of a severe storm and before the next system arrives. Wind gusts near 60 mph and quarter-size hail can cause hidden damage — lifted fasteners and bruised shingles — that is not visible from the ground. Waiting lets small leaks spread and gives insurers room to attribute damage to age. A prompt, dated inspection captures the condition close to the storm event, which matters for any future claim.

### Can 60 mph wind really damage my roof if I see no missing shingles?
Yes — 60 mph gusts can cause damage similar to a weak tornado even when no shingles are obviously gone. Wind lifts and reseats shingle tabs, breaking the adhesive seal and fasteners underneath, so the roof looks intact but is no longer watertight. It also loosens ridge caps and flashing, common failure points on elevated homes in Keller and Flower Mound. An AI drone inspection can spot these subtle lifts and seal failures that a ground view misses.

### What does hail damage actually look like on an asphalt roof?
Hail damage appears as dark, bruised spots where granules are knocked loose, exposing the black asphalt mat underneath. You may see circular dents on soft metal like vents, gutters, and flashing, plus heavy granule accumulation at downspouts. Quarter-size hail, like the recent North Texas event, is large enough to fracture aging shingles in older Richardson and Garland neighborhoods. Since fresh hail bruises fade over time, photograph suspected damage quickly and have it documented.

### Should I file an insurance claim before or after getting an inspection?
Get a documented inspection first, then decide on the claim. An inspection with dated photographs and a line-item estimate tells you whether the damage exceeds your deductible and gives you evidence the adjuster can verify. Filing blindly on minor damage can count against you, while filing without documentation weakens your case. ### Why would my insurer blame storm damage on wear and tear?
Insurers may classify damage as age-related when a roof is old or poorly maintained, which reduces or denies the payout. Brittle, sun-degraded shingles and deteriorated flashing can resemble wear even when a storm caused the fresh damage — a frequent issue on 1990s homes in Bedford and Carrollton. A professional inspection distinguishes new storm damage from pre-existing deterioration. Keeping your roof repaired and documented before storms removes the ammunition for a wear-based denial.

### Is my roof at higher risk because I live along the I-35 corridor?
Yes — the recent storm system intensified as it moved toward the I-35 corridor by 10 p.m., exposing those communities to the strongest gusts. Suburbs from Denton through Fort Worth sit in the common track of fast-moving cold fronts that generate wind and hail. Rapid-growth areas like Frisco and McKinney add risk because so many roofs there are now 15 to 20 years old. If you're along this corridor, inspect after every significant severe-weather event.

### What should I do if water is already leaking into my house?
Contain the water immediately and arrange emergency roof tarping to stop further intrusion. Move belongings, place buckets under active drips, and photograph interior stains on ceilings and attic decking for your records. Flash flooding and wind-driven rain exploit small roof openings, so a leak often signals compromised flashing or lifted shingles above. Call a licensed contractor for temporary protection, then schedule permanent roof repair once the roof is dry and safe.
