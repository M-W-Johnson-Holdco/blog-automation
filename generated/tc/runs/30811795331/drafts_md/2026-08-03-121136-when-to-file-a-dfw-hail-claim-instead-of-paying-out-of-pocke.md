# When to File a DFW Hail Claim Instead of Paying Out of Pocket

Texas insurance code 28 TAC 21.1006 lets your carrier require roughly 25 percent of your total roof area to show storm-related damage before authorizing a full replacement on a claim (Source: [roofraidersandco.com](https://roofraidersandco.com/services/roof-repair-dallas), n.d.). That single threshold explains why two neighbors on the same Plano street can file identical hail claims after the same storm — and one receives a full replacement check while the other gets a spot-repair letter. For Dallas-Fort Worth homeowners with aging 1990s tract-built roofs, knowing whether to file a claim or pay out of pocket comes down to documentation and roof age, not the storm's headline severity.

**The short answer:** File a claim when your roof carries documented storm damage from a carrier-honored date, is over 18 years old, or sits in the hail belt one storm from total loss — pay out of pocket only for isolated wear unrelated to a covered event.
**Who this affects:** Homeowners across Dallas, Tarrant, Collin, and Denton counties, especially those with 1980s–2000s asphalt shingle roofs approaching the end of their lifespan.
**What to do:** Get a professional roof inspection that documents damage to the carrier's own language before deciding to file or self-fund a repair.
**Timeline:** Before your next policy renewal, and again at your annual inspection.

## Is a repair worth the money on an aging DFW roof?

A repair is wasted money when your roof already carries covered storm damage, has outlived its underlayment, or sits one major storm from total loss. Industry guidance identifies three scenarios where a cash repair is bad math: pre-existing storm damage an insurance claim should cover, roofs over 18 years old with multiple repair invoices on file, and hail-belt homes one storm from replacement (Source: [roofraidersandco.com](https://roofraidersandco.com/services/roof-repair-dallas), n.d.). Many Richardson and Garland roofs built in the 1980s and 1990s have already weathered multiple hail seasons, thinning granule coverage to the point that a fresh event tips a repairable roof into a full loss. The practical takeaway: before spending on a fall patch, confirm whether a documented claim is the smarter path for a roof already near the end of its service life.

## How do I know if a marginal storm actually damaged my roof?

Storm coverage depends on whether the damage is storm-caused rather than pre-existing wear — not on the storm's official risk level. A lower-category storm can still produce hail and gusty winds capable of damaging an aging Tarrant County roof, and insurers evaluate the damage itself, not the Storm Prediction Center rating (Source: [tcroofingexperts.com](https://www.tcroofingexperts.com/blog/p.260714000/north-texas-marginal-risk-storm-sunday-is-your-dfw-roof-ready), n.d.). The suburbs along the US-75 corridor — running from McKinney south through Frisco, Plano, and Richardson — sit in DFW's heaviest hail path during the April-through-June peak season. Documenting your roof's condition before a storm makes it far easier to prove new damage came from a specific event, which is why a baseline professional roof inspection protects your ability to file later.

## What does the 25 percent damage threshold mean for my claim?

The 25 percent threshold means a carrier can require roughly a quarter of your total roof area to show storm-related damage before approving a full roof replacement rather than a spot repair. This is why documentation determines outcomes: a roof is measured to the carrier's own language, with test-square chalk marks on every slope photographed with a measuring tape in frame, hail strikes counted per 100 square feet, and soft metals like vents, gutter aprons, and ridge caps photographed for impact bruising (Source: [roofraidersandco.com](https://roofraidersandco.com/services/roof-repair-dallas), n.d.). For a homeowner in Allen or Wylie whose roof genuinely crossed that threshold, sloppy documentation can still produce a spot-repair letter. The takeaway: insist your contractor cross-references collateral damage on screens, fascia, and gutters — the paperwork, not the storm, decides the payout.
