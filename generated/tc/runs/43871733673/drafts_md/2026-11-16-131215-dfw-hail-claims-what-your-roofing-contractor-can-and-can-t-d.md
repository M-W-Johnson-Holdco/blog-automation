## FAQ

### Can a roofing contractor in Frisco negotiate my insurance settlement for me?

No — under Texas law, a roofing contractor cannot negotiate your settlement or act as your public adjuster; that work requires a separate state license. A contractor's proper role is documenting the damage and producing a priced repair estimate, while a licensed public adjuster handles negotiation. If a roofing company in Frisco offers to negotiate your claim directly, treat that as a warning sign. The cleanest setup is a partnership where the contractor and adjuster each stay in their lane.

### Do I need a public adjuster for my single-family roof claim in Mesquite?

For a straightforward single-family roof claim, you often do not need a public adjuster. Independent representation matters most on large or complicated losses — multiple buildings, HOA and multifamily master policies, denied or delayed claims, and anything involving business interruption. A single roof in Mesquite or Rowlett typically falls below that complexity threshold. Still document thoroughly, keep damaged materials until an adjuster sees them, and get a dated contractor estimate to support the claim.

### How should I document roof damage before a crew starts cleanup?

Photograph everything before a tarp goes up or a crew hauls away debris — roof, siding, HVAC, interior water damage, and any inventory or business-interruption costs. Keep the damaged materials on site until the adjuster has physically inspected them. A dated photo record paired with a contractor's line-item estimate is the strongest evidence you can give an insurer. Skipping this step is where many North Texas claims go wrong.

### Why does DFW have two hail seasons instead of one?

Dallas-Fort Worth sits in a weather pattern that produces spring storms peaking April through June, followed by a second severe-weather window in October and November. That dual pattern creates two annual filing waves rather than one. For property owners, this means a roof can be struck twice in a single year. The practical response is inspecting after each window — spring and fall — rather than relying on a once-a-year check.

### My ceiling tile stained months after a storm — is it too late to file?

A delayed interior stain does not automatically mean it's too late, but you should act quickly to document the connection between the storm and the damage. This is a common North Texas pattern — a roof that looks fine from the parking lot, then a ceiling tile stains six months later. Photograph the interior damage immediately and arrange a roof inspection to trace the leak source and granule loss back to hail impact. The sooner you document, the stronger the link between the storm event and the loss.

### Why are large multifamily hail claims in Plano so complicated?

Large multifamily claims are complex because scope becomes the entire fight — a complex with damage across a dozen buildings under a master policy involves far more documentation than a single roof. For a 200-unit complex, the scope is the whole game. In Plano's Tollway-corridor retail and apartment stock, missing damage on even a few buildings can cost a property owner substantially. These are the cases where a public adjuster's independent representation most often pays off.

### What's the difference between what a contractor and an adjuster document?

A contractor documents and prices the physical repair, while a public adjuster documents and negotiates the overall loss. These are distinct, legally separate roles under Texas law — the contractor cannot cross into negotiation, and the adjuster handles the settlement dispute. Keeping them separate protects the integrity of your claim. The ideal arrangement is a coordinated partnership where each professional contributes their licensed expertise.

### Should I inspect my roof in Arlington even if I don't see obvious damage?

Yes — hail bruising and granule loss are frequently invisible from the ground, so an Arlington or Grand Prairie roof can carry real damage that looks fine on a quick glance. Delayed damage often surfaces later as interior staining once water finds its way through compromised shingles. A drone-based roof inspection captures the full surface and flags bruising and flashing issues before they become leaks. Scheduling an inspection after each severe-weather window is far cheaper than repairing water damage later.
