## What Do RCV and ACV Mean for Your Payout?

RCV means Replacement Cost Value — what it costs to replace your roof with new materials today — while ACV means Actual Cash Value, the depreciated figure after age and wear are subtracted. The difference matters because a claim strategy focused on reducing payouts can lean on depreciation, exclusions, or narrow damage scoping to shrink the number you receive. A deductible is the amount you pay before coverage applies, and it comes off the top of whatever valuation the insurer uses. When an adjuster's scope misses damaged flashing, soft metals, or granule loss across slopes that weren't closely examined, the gap between a fair RCV roof replacement and the offered amount widens quickly. Ask your insurer in writing whether your settlement is RCV or ACV, and confirm which roof components the adjuster actually inspected.
